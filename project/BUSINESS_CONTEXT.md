# MTL-XF-028 — Business context

## Organisation and decision owner

**Simulated client:** Confidential Combined Authority Net Zero & Programme Investment Board. **Accountable executive:** Director of Net Zero & Climate Programme Investment. **Review cadence:** annual emissions baseline review and quarterly programme-prioritisation meeting.

This fictional directorate/board is an engagement scenario, not an assertion of a real client's circumstances. The source data remains official public/open data.

## Business problem

The combined authority must choose which sector/place opportunities deserve feasibility work, but the largest territorial emission source may lie outside its direct influence. Changes caused by national electricity supply or industrial activity can also dominate a local trend without demonstrating local policy success. A defensible investment discussion needs a consistent inventory and a separate assessment of delivery influence.

## Why the current process is insufficient

The current scenario relies on separately prepared reports without a common source/metric contract. The material failure to prevent is specific: Local influence, inventory revisions and gas/unit differences can create misleading decarbonisation priorities.

This creates uncertainty over which cases need investigation and which comparisons should be rejected. No quantified loss, backlog, budget deficit or service failure is invented as a client fact.

## Commissioned decision

Which local-authority and sector combinations warrant decarbonisation programme investigation based on emissions scale, trend and realistic local influence?

## Practical management use case

The Director compares a high-emissions LA/transport combination with an area whose emissions are falling slowly. The team opens the sector bridge, checks whether the change is structural, and records whether local programme levers are plausible. The shortlist commissions feasibility and cost work; it does not approve investment solely from a carbon ranking.

## Current-to-target workflow

1. **Before review:** analyst registers the approved files, coverage and definitions; steward checks the cohort and unresolved exceptions.
2. **At review:** Director of Net Zero & Climate Programme Investment opens the overview, then inspects diagnostics and the underlying eligible population before considering a case.
3. **Challenge:** the relevant stakeholder compares the alternative scenario and asks whether the signal survives missingness, comparability and denominator checks.
4. **Action:** each accepted case records investigation type, responsible service role, missing evidence, next review and what finding would reverse the recommendation.
5. **Follow-up:** the next annual emissions baseline review and quarterly programme-prioritisation meeting records evidence received and the decision, without claiming the analytical signal itself caused an improvement.

## Required analytical response

- Build territorial emissions trend and sector contribution bridges, reconciling totals at declared hierarchy.
- Compare 2005 baseline and recent five-year patterns; isolate boundary changes and negative/net emission categories.
- Prioritise up to ten LA/sector investigation cases using burden, trend and a documented local-influence assessment; test at least two weight sets.

## Measurable project outcomes

1. Every carbon result identifies gas/CO2e scope, mass unit, inventory version and geography.
2. Every priority case has a separate local-influence justification and cost/feasibility evidence request.
3. Both weight sets disclose component values and changes in shortlisted cases.

## Proposed benefit monitoring

At kickoff, record a baseline review process and propose measures for: time to regenerate the agreed evidence pack; proportion of agenda recommendations with a complete evidence chain; and proportion of accepted investigations with a role owner and next review. The target for recommendation traceability and ownership is 100%; regeneration time is measured during the independent rerun, with no fabricated pre-project baseline or claimed reduction. These are proposed management measures, not achieved service benefits.

## Scope and evidence limits

**Core:** Agreed DESNZ workbook, 2005–2024. Default screening covers English LA/sector records; recent comparison uses 2019–2024, with 2005 as the long-run reference where a valid positive baseline exists. Use a single inventory revision and boundary basis. Territorial total greenhouse gases remain separate from any narrower local-influence measure. Present at most ten shortlisted LA/sector cases for feasibility investigation.

**Extensions:** Cost per tonne, investment returns, consumption-based emissions, future target delivery and causal local-policy attribution need additional evidence. Confirm the gas scope of within-influence tables before comparing with total GHG; do not assume they share coverage.

- Territorial emissions and emissions within local influence are different scopes.
- Historical reductions do not prove a local policy caused them; investment recommendations need cost and feasibility evidence.
- Treat negative/net emissions and land-use categories explicitly; no unqualified percentage ranking on nonpositive baselines.

## Risks, mitigations and escalation

- **Interpretation risk:** Local influence, inventory revisions and gas/unit differences can create misleading decarbonisation priorities. The domain specialist reviews definitions and DT1–DT10 before sign-off.
- **Source risk:** Workbook listing, revision/boundary context and scope distinction verified; sheet-level mappings, precise gas/unit compatibility and frozen checksums pending. The steward records unresolved items at G1; mandatory gaps block dependent outputs.
- **Decision risk:** prioritisation assumptions may affect which cases appear. Show alternative scenarios, excluded cases and evidence that could change a recommendation.
- **Delivery risk:** limited time or skills can leave undocumented manual steps. Assign a second-member rerun and retain contribution/review evidence throughout the eight weeks.

## Evidence chain

Stakeholder requirement → registered source and valid cohort → governed field/metric → tested transformation → reconciled finding → scenario reason code → investigation owner/evidence request → simulated UAT and sign-off.

Every major recommendation must be traceable through this chain.
